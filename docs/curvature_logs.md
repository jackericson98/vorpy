# Curvature perspectives in CSV logs

The edge section uses the numbered Ball columns as its reference identifiers.
“Integrated Mean Curvature (Ball 1)” refers to the cell generated by the
identifier in that row's “Ball 1” column, not necessarily atom index 1.
All edge contributions use the selected cell's outward face normals and its
induced face-boundary orientation. They do not depend on which endpoint is
listed first.

For each physical edge the log records:

- Three cell-relative integrated mean-curvature contributions.
- Three cell-relative integrated Gaussian edge contributions. These are
  geodesic-curvature boundary integrals, not smooth-surface Gaussian curvature.
- Six directed face-incidence Gaussian contributions. For example, “Ball 1,
  Face with Ball 2” is the boundary integral on the shared face from Ball 1's
  cell perspective. The two entries for a cell sum to its edge contribution.

An individual cell's mean edge term is generally different from the terms of
the other two cells. For a union containing two of the three generating cells,
the mean edge term uses the negative of the excluded cell's term. This is a
complement rule, not a way to obtain either other individual cell's value.
For union Gaussian bookkeeping, select the directed incidences belonging to
the retained outward boundary faces; do not apply the mean-curvature sign rule.

Blank entries indicate unavailable curvature, rather than zero. Surrounding
support cells can have local edge contributions even when their entire cells
are not closed or evaluated as target cells.

The redundant “Integrated Mean Curvature (Face)” column is removed from the
Surfaces section. Its existing “Integrated Mean Curvature” column retains the
native implicit-surface orientation. Group and atom face/edge decompositions
remain separate, since those fields represent distinct contributions.

Older aggregate-only edge logs remain readable, but their summed values cannot
recover the individual cell perspectives without recalculating the geometry.
