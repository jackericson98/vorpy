Construction trace
------------------
`Network.build()` performs spatial indexing/sorting, `find_verts()`,
`connect()`, `build_edges()`, `build_surfaces()`, and then either `analyze()`
or `analyze_geometry_only()`. The selected `net.group` controls which cells

vertex when it has at least one selected generator; interface discovery

`Network.build()`; those are opt-in command analysis after primal geometry.

The completeness predicate is strict: every selected cell needs at least
three vertices, four edges, and three surfaces; every vertex must have the
expected three incident cell edges or three locally supported subset edges;
AW unresolved analytic vertices also invalidate the cell in full analysis.
There is no rule that removes a cell merely because it contacts solvent.

Incremental group completion milestone
=======================================

Date: 2026-10-19. This bounded prototype does not alter CLI, archive, export,
or EDTA construction paths. It accepts a solved source network and a requested
group, then either returns the existing exact `NetworkView`, materializes only
target-supported vertices plus exhaustively discovered bounded candidates, or
returns/raises an explicit normal-build-required result.

Reuse and dependency inventory
------------------------------

Vertices are reusable when their four-generator incidence, stable generator
IDs, location, radius, and completeness are valid and at least one generator
belongs to the requested group. Edges and surfaces are not copied blindly:
they are rebuilt from reused/candidate vertices, preserving incidence and
stable endpoint/face identities. Group cell measurements are compared after
reanalysis. Reuse requires matching scheme, full physical generator universe,
positions/radii, boundary configuration and IDs, solvent/context membership,
resolution, solver/build settings, and solver version. Source group membership
is deliberately excluded from the compatibility comparison so Group C can be
derived from a compatible A/B source.

Candidate validation enumerates all four-generator combinations only within a
bounded generator/combination budget and verifies every candidate against the
complete source competitor universe, including retained solvent/context
generators. AW uses both roots; PRM uses primitive distance; POW uses the
production squared-power bound. Incomplete source incidence is recorded as
evidence and never promoted to complete.

Direct/full route
-----------------

`Interface.make_net()` calls `prepare_network_geometry()` over the complete
physical generator universe selected by solvent mode, including water/ions in
`FULL_SOLVENT` and `SOLVENT_CONTEXT`, plus explicit auxiliary boundary rows.
`Network.find_verts()` and `build_net()` use all competitor coordinates but

solve, not a full-system topology materialization. A full-system topology is
constructed only for an explicit full-source request; `_attach_full_system_interface_views()` then uses `NetworkView` without rebuilding geometry.

The prior bounded evidence is feature-level parity for AW/POW/PRM: A/B
facets, generator pairs, area, edge/vertex incidence, completeness evidence,

and closure checks are covered separately. Universal CLI full-source parity

Water-facing surfaces are classified as support, not direct A/B area.
Selected surface closure still retains required water/boundary-supported
edges and vertices; exported interface owners remain A/B while surrounding
exports may include closure atoms. Ion retention/removal remains policy input,
not an inferred solver decision.

Scaling audit
------------

* Direct construction allocates O(N) physical location/radius/mass and
  metadata storage. FULL_SOLVENT therefore remains O(N) in solvent count even
  when only a small A/B topology is retained.
* Full-source mode materializes O(N) topology and meshes before O(F) view
  selection. It is not selective geometry construction.
* AW verification has a material small-system risk: for at most 4096
  generators, each candidate can scan the full location/radius arrays; larger
  cases use spatial neighborhoods. The sparse AW recovery fallback is O(N^4)
  but is gated to at most 32 generators.
* POW/PRM use local spatial neighborhoods for candidate verification and flat
  surfaces, reducing curvature/mesh memory but not the O(N) competitor arrays.
* Surface meshing retains point/triangle/curvature arrays per constructed
  facet; AW curvature is the dominant memory risk. No operator benchmark was
  launched.

Checkpoint contract
-------------------

The canonical writer is staged before optional logs, meshes, and export
writers. Full-source CLI geometry is also persisted before source-derived
views. However, direct interface builds persist only when control reaches the
export/checkpoint stage: `Interface.build()` performs geometry analysis and,
when requested, water analysis before that handoff. A failure in either stage
can therefore leave valid `Network` geometry without a durable canonical
checkpoint. This is a PRISM/VECTOR/ATLAS coordination item, not fixed under
the freeze. The old internal `.geometry.vpy` path can also be redundant with
the canonical checkpoint.
